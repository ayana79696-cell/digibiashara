# DigiBiashara Business Catalogue Blueprint

## Product rule

Registration must model **what the business does**, not only its name. A business selects one primary category, then one or more subcategories/activities. The workspace is assembled from the union of the compatible feature modules and product/service templates. Do not create separate accounts or duplicate inventory when activities belong to the same business.

The catalogue below is the initial 150-type planning list for Kenya. Names are user-facing options; they should be reviewed against the Kenya Standard Industrial Classification (KeSIC) and sector-specific requirements before the catalogue is treated as a formal economic classification.

## Registration and setup flow

1. Choose a main category.
2. Select one or more business types/activities under it.
3. Show a plain-language preview of the modules that will be enabled.
4. Create the business profile and starter categories/templates only after confirmation.
5. Let the owner edit starter products/services and enter their actual prices, stock, suppliers and staff.
6. Support adding activities later without losing existing records.
7. Allow custom products, services, units and categories, with validation appropriate to the business.
8. Keep specialised modules (healthcare, education, finance, regulated products) permission-restricted and clearly marked as requiring further compliance review.

## Shared platform capabilities

- Business account, staff, roles and permissions
- Customers/clients and suppliers
- Sales, invoices/receipts, refunds and discounts
- Expenses and payment records
- Inventory, stock movements and stock counts where relevant
- Reports, notifications, audit history and business settings
- Configurable payment methods and tax/invoice integration points

These shared capabilities are enabled only where appropriate. A service-only business should not be forced to manage stock; an inventory business should not be forced to use appointments.

## Feature and template rules by activity

### General retail / supermarket
- Suggested departments: groceries, household products, personal care, beverages and other editable departments.
- Units: piece, packet, bottle, carton, kilogram, litre; allow per-product unit selection.
- Features: barcode-ready product lookup, checkout, discounts, returns, stock counts, supplier purchases, low-stock alerts, daily sales and margin reports.
- Only suggest food/regulated product tracking where relevant. Do not preload fixed stock quantities or prices.

### Cereals and grains
- Suggested product groups: maize, rice, beans, green grams, wheat, millet, sorghum and other grains, all editable.
- Units: kg, g, 50 kg sack and custom units; support fractional quantities and unit conversion with explicit configured conversion factors.
- Features: weighing-based checkout, purchase cost, stock reconciliation, packaging/processing loss and margin reporting.

### Salon / barber / nails / spa
- Separate service catalogue from retail products.
- Hair salon starter services may include braiding, washing, treatment, styling and wig installation; owners edit services, durations and prices.
- Nail/spa activities add nail treatments, massage and appointment calendars as selected.
- Features: appointments and walk-ins, staff/service assignment, configurable commission, customer visit history, consumable usage, product sales, deposits and daily service reports.
- Product examples: wigs, extensions, braids, shampoo, conditioner, oils, treatments and accessories. These are suggestions, not mandatory inventory.

### Butchery / poultry / fish
- Units: kg, g, piece and custom unit; support weighed quantity and price calculation.
- Features: supplier intake, stock by product/cut, configurable yield, spoilage/wastage records, stock reconciliation and margin reporting.
- Do not treat wastage as a sale or silently assume all purchased weight is saleable.

### Electronics / mobile / repair
- Product variants and serial/IMEI capture where appropriate; do not require serial numbers for every accessory.
- Features: warranty information, supplier records, repairs/work orders when selected, device/customer details, spare parts, deposits and repair status.
- Keep device identifiers and customer data access-controlled.

### Clothing / boutique / footwear
- Variants: size, colour, design/style and optional SKU/barcode.
- Features: variant-level stock, exchanges/returns, wholesale price tiers if wholesale is selected, supplier purchases and sales by category.

### Baby shop
- Suggested groups: diapers, baby clothing, feeding, bathing, nursery, toys and baby-care products.
- Variants: size, age range, colour and pack size where relevant.
- Features: variant stock, expiry tracking for applicable products, supplier management and retail/wholesale prices if selected.

### Agrovet / farm inputs
- Product groups: seeds, fertilisers, animal feeds, crop protection and veterinary products as permitted for the operator.
- Features: batch/lot, expiry, supplier, stock movements and product-specific records where applicable.
- Regulated products must not be assumed lawful to sell merely because the category is selected; compliance and permissions need sector review.

### Restaurant / café / food service
- Separate menu items from retail stock; recipes/ingredients and portion sizes are configurable.
- Features: tables or takeaway orders where needed, order status, modifiers, kitchen tickets, ingredient deduction if configured, wastage, daily sales and staff shifts.
- Hotel and catering activities can be combined without duplicating the business account.

### Hotel / accommodation / venue
- Features: room/venue types, availability, reservations, check-in/out, deposits, balances, housekeeping/maintenance status and occupancy reports.
- Restaurant, conference and event modules may be added as activities under the same business.

### Healthcare
- Potential modules: patient registration, appointments, clinical notes, prescriptions, laboratory requests, pharmacy and billing, each with least-privilege access and audit trail.
- This is a sensitive, regulated domain. Do not enable a production clinical-record workflow until the relevant Kenyan health, data-protection, interoperability and certification requirements have been assessed.

### Education
- Potential modules: learner records, admissions, attendance, timetables, fees, assessment/results and guardian communication, adapted to institution type.
- Student and child records require strict access controls and privacy review.

### Property / rentals
- Features: properties/units, tenants, lease terms, rent schedules, payment ledger, arrears, maintenance tickets, deposits and statements.

### Transport / fleet / logistics
- Features: vehicles, drivers, trips/jobs, route or delivery status, fuel and maintenance costs, customer billing and fleet reports.
- Vehicle, driver and trip records must be separate from ordinary inventory.

### Agriculture / livestock
- Features vary by activity: farm/plot records, production cycles, inputs, harvests, animal groups, feed, health events, sales and operating costs.
- Do not assume crop, dairy, poultry and fish farming have identical workflows.

### Manufacturing
- Features: raw materials, bills of materials/recipes, production batches, yield, wastage, finished goods, quality checks and cost estimates.
- Only activate recipe/BOM workflows when configured by the owner.

### Professional / digital / repair services
- Features: enquiries, quotations, projects or jobs, milestones, timesheets where useful, expenses, invoices, recurring clients and service profitability.
- Inventory is optional unless the business sells goods or uses tracked parts/materials.

### SACCO / insurance / other financial services
- Treat these as specialist business profiles, not a promise that the platform itself is a licensed financial service.
- Financial member records, lending, policy and payment workflows require a separate regulatory and security assessment before implementation.

## Combination rules

- A business may select multiple activities, including activities across main categories.
- Activate the union of selected modules; deduplicate shared modules.
- Keep one stock ledger unless the owner explicitly configures separate locations/branches.
- Do not merge incompatible product records merely because names match; preserve unit, SKU, batch and other relevant identity.
- Allow the owner to add an activity later; show what new features will be enabled before applying changes.
- A module can be marked required, optional, unavailable pending compliance review, or planned for a later release.
- A custom business type should use a safe generic profile and allow the owner to choose capabilities; never invent a specialised workflow for an unsupported type.
